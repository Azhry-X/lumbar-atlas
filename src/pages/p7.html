1 · Spinal Manipulative Therapy (SMT), Mobilisation & NICE NG59 NICE NG59PACKAGE WITH EXERCISE
NICE manual therapy NICE NG59: “Consider manual therapy (spinal manipulation, mobilisation or soft tissue techniques such as massage) … but only as part of a treatment package that includes exercise” (1.2.7, amended 2026) — Level: Guideline
SMT for chronic LBP (47 RCTs, n = 9,211) (Rubinstein 2019 BMJ) M: similar effect to other recommended therapies for short-term pain: MD −3.17 (−7.85 to 1.51), moderate quality
Manipulation CPR (see page 4): Flynn 2002 (5 criteria; ≥ 4/5 → LR+ 24.38; probability of success 45% → 95% L) • Childs 2004 (rule-positive + manipulation: OR 60.8 (5.2–704.7); 92% chance of success; NNT 1.9 (1.4–3.5) at 4 weeks M)
2 · Massage (Cochrane — Furlan 2015) SHORT-TERM ONLY
Massage (Cochrane) (Furlan 2015) L: subacute/chronic vs inactive control, short-term pain SMD −0.75 (−0.90 to −0.60), function SMD −0.72 (−1.05 to −0.39); not in long term
Per NICE NG59 (1.2.7, amended 2026), deliver soft-tissue massage only as part of a treatment package that includes exercise.
3 · Lumbar Stenosis RCT (Schneider 2019) NON-SURGICAL RCT
Stenosis — non-surgical (RCT, n = 259) (Schneider 2019 JAMA Netw Open) M: medical care vs group exercise vs manual therapy + individual exercise
Integrates spine/hip mobilisation with individualised conditioning and walking progression.

5 · Pre-Manipulation & Manual Therapy Safety ScreenCONTRAINDICATION GATE
Cauda equina syndrome excluded: bladder, bowel & saddle sensation intact (p1) L
Vertebral fracture & osteoporosis excluded: trauma / steroid / multi-flag screen negative (p1) M
Spinal malignancy & infection excluded: cancer history & systemic fever/infection negative (p1) M
Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) excluded: no expansile abdominal mass or vascular pain (p1) M
No progressive neurological deficit: serial myotomes & reflexes stable (p3) E
Anticoagulant / bleeding / post-op fusion hardware screened: no thrust over unstable/fused segments E
6 · Dry Needling Safety & Anatomical Danger Zones SAFETY SCREEN
Adverse event rates: Brady 2014 M: mild AEs 19.18 %; upper risk rate for significant AEs ≤ 0.04 %; bruising 7.55 %, bleeding 4.65 % • Boyce 2020 M: minor AE 36.7 %; major AE < .1 % (prolonged symptom aggravation .03 %, fainting .02 %, forgotten needles .01 %; major AEs most often with gluteal, lumbar paraspinal, suboccipital and upper trapezius needling)
Serious risks & screening: Halle 2016 E: pneumothorax, cardiac tamponade, haematoma, infection, CNS injury; filament needles 32–36 gauge (.25–.20 mm) • Kearns 2019 E: anticoagulants / coagulopathy — caution — possible increased incidence or severity of haematoma and bruising
Lumbar / pelvic danger zones: Valera-Calero 2024 L: piriformis over the sciatic nerve — deeper fascia depth 7.0 ± 1.3 cm • Halle 2016 E: quadratus lumborum — the kidney lies towards the lateral portion of quadratus lumborum
