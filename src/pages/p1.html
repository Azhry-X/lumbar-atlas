Cauda Equina Syndrome (CES) — Same-Day EmergencySurgical Emergency
Cauda equina syndrome — activate same-day emergency pathway for MRI & neurosurgical / spinal review
Urinary retention: Sn 0.90, Sp 0.99 (classic estimate, Deyo 1992 JAMA) L
Bladder scan rule: PVR > 200 mL → MRI-confirmed CES in 43%; PVR < 200 mL without objective signs → MRI+ rare (Ann R Coll Surg Engl 2024) L • PVR: mL
Timing & prognosis: decompression < 48 h associated with better outcomes (Ahn 2000 meta-analysis) L; bladder status at the time of surgery is the dominant factor for bladder recovery (Qureshi & Sell 2007) L

Malignancy, Vascular, Inflammatory & InfectionSystemic & NICE NG59
Malignancy red flag (Downie 2013 BMJ) M: history of cancer 7% (3–16) in primary care; 33% (22–46) in emergency care
Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA palpation, Fink 2000 Arch Intern Med) M: Sn 68% (60–76), Sp 75% (68–82), LR+ 2.7, LR− 0.43; Sn 82% for AAA ≥ 5.0 cm; Sn 91% if girth < 100 cm vs 53% if ≥ 100 cm
Axial spondyloarthritis (ASAS criteria, Rudwaleit 2011) M: Sn 79.5%, Sp 83.3% • chronic inflammatory back pain → rheumatology referral
Spinal infection E: fever/rigors, IV drug use, immunosuppression, recent spinal procedure, relentless non-mechanical pain → urgent medical workup
Imaging rule NICE NG59: “Do not routinely offer imaging in a non-specialist setting” (1.1.4); consider imaging in specialist settings “only if the result is likely to change management” (1.1.6) • ref: Jarvik & Deyo 2002

Symptom Location & Clinical PatternClinical Signpost

Distal Dermatomal Leg Pain > Back Pain: lancinating radiation with paresthesia → Radiculopathy / disc herniation (p4)

Alternating Buttock Pain & Morning Stiffness Relieved by Movement: insidious onset → Axial spondyloarthritis screen (p1/p2)
Mechanical Aggravators & Easing FactorsBehaviour
• Sustained flexion / sitting / lifting: Discogenic / radicular
• Sustained standing / walking / extension: Stenosis / facet / pars
• Cough / sneeze / Valsalva impulse: Dural / disc root irritation
• Sit-to-stand / rolling in bed: Segmental instability / SIJ
• Walking / gentle movement eases pain: Mechanical / inflammatory
• Seated flexion relieves leg symptoms: Neurogenic claudication
